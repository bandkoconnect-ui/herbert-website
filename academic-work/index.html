Designing complex sytems

Extensive communication capabilities are a fact of the contemporary world and have very strongly influenced design solutions, creating powerful, unlocalized systems of specialized objects working in network. Meanwhile, advances in the efficiency of material and energy consumption gave rise to extremely multifunctional devices, complex systems within the object that don’t have to rely on external structures to accomplish a great variety of tasks. Both contradictory and complementary in nature, these distinct sides of the integration paradigm can be observed very pronouncedly in the industrial, interface and service design fields and testifies to the human flexibility in understanding the world around her/himself. Through this flexibility, our society finds ways to integrate objects to maximize efficiency, well-being, entertainment, communication and enable experiences, all at the same time.

 

Between its promises and perils, this thesis studies the mechanisms that facilitate or restrict the development and use of these complex systems of objects, these boundless objects, through the description and exploration of the nature of objects in term of how we understand them as fractal relationships, and the role of design, designers, and users in defining said systems.

 

This is a matter of acknowledging the relationship between objects and of them with their users, which ultimately defines the role of design and designers in the active and intentional shaping of things.

Boundless Design theory

This master’s thesis explored the process of understanding from a theoretical and philosophical perspective, examining its impact on design. Through this research, I realized that our perception—shaped by orientation, distance, and time—affects how we interpret objects. In essence, we don’t interact with objects themselves but with our understanding of them.

Ideation and concepts

Using the ideas and conclusions from the theoretical study, combined with the results from the case study, I began designing a variety of applications for them, ranging from physical objects to interfaces, identity and service concepts.

Many were the tools used to study these projects. Hand drawing, wireframing, and 3D modeling were amongst them. Still, they were always initiated and guided by the theoretical findings.
